About

Much of my work has centered on the spaces between categories — where a neighborhood meets a market, a building meets its landscape, or one kind of use gives way to another.

I treat architecture less as a finished object than as a frame for how people actually live: the small, ordinary acts that give a place its texture. Across my work — a thesis that reworked the boundary between a commercial district and its residents into a shared market, a kindergarten that lets its walls dissolve into the surrounding forest, a campus building that organizes movement while echoing the material memory of the architecture around it — I keep returning to one question: how space is shaped by the people who use it, and how it shapes them back. Three years of practice in Los Angeles have grounded that instinct in real constraints; my focus now turns toward civic and public work, where everyday life carries the highest stakes.

Approach

Context-driven designWorking with site, code, budget, culture, and everyday use as active design materials rather than limitations.
