About

Much of my work has centered on the spaces between categories — where a neighborhood meets a market, a building meets its landscape, or one kind of use gives way to another.

I treat architecture less as a finished object than as a frame for how people actually live: the small, ordinary acts that give a place its texture. Across my work — a thesis that reworked the boundary between a commercial district and its residents into a shared market, a kindergarten that lets its walls dissolve into the surrounding forest, a campus building that organizes movement while echoing the material memory of the architecture around it — I keep returning to one question: how space is shaped by the people who use it, and how it shapes them back.

At Dotz in Los Angeles, I joined as one of the founding designers of the in-house design team. I now run 20+ projects at a time, each end to end: design, construction documents, consultants and plan check. Recent work includes Beck, a 20-unit, 100% affordable building taken from schematic design through final approval under CHIP; Westfall, a hillside SB 9 house that nearly doubles its usable area; and Ashton, a two-story remodel with a rooftop terrace and a detached ADU.

Before that, I worked on large-scale commercial projects for South Korea at CallisonRTKL in Los Angeles. I hold an M.Arch from USC and a B.Arch from Beijing University of Civil Engineering and Architecture. Three years of practice have grounded my instinct for everyday life in real constraints; my focus now turns toward civic and public work, where everyday life carries the highest stakes.
